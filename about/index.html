About

Most of what I build starts with one question: how does this actually work?

I study computer engineering at Georgia Tech, on the Cybersecurity and Distributed Systems & Software Design threads, and I expect to graduate in May 2028. What draws me to engineering is the layer underneath: not just what a program does, but how it moves data, where it spends its time, and what the hardware is doing while it runs.

That curiosity is easiest to see in my projects. I wrote a 3D renderer from scratch in Python and NumPy, with no graphics API, so I could work through every stage myself: projection, backface culling, clipping, depth ordering and lighting. In Unity I wrote a thermal-vision post-processing shader in HLSL that maps scene depth to a cold-to-hot gradient and outlines objects with Sobel edge detection. I also studied GPU programming in China, which strengthened my interest in how hardware and software work together to improve performance.

Finance gave that interest a practical direction. As a Portfolio Manager in the Georgia Tech Student Foundation Investment Committee's Quant Sector, I've built a Python portfolio controller that coordinates capital allocation, rebalancing and risk constraints. I've refactored our backtesting infrastructure to support train/test separation across multiple strategies, and integrated two independent trading strategies into one execution system. Working on a portfolio manager and tools for organizing historical stock data showed me how much good software engineering matters when the problem is financial.

Before Georgia Tech, I was president of Chess Buddies, an organization that teaches chess to underprivileged children across Metro Atlanta. I led a six-person volunteer team that hosted more than 60 events each school year at two schools, reaching more than 50 children. Leading that team taught me to plan, delegate and explain ideas clearly to people who are just starting out.

Along the way I've been a two-time AIME qualifier, received the Zell Miller Scholarship and made the Dean's List. I've also placed first in a 50-person trading simulation, placed third in a betting simulation, and been a semifinalist in the McKinsey Case Competition.

Outside of class I play golf, basketball, pickleball and poker, rock climb, listen to music, and travel when I get the chance.
